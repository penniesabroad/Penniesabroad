3 Days in Glasgow on a Budget: 5 Things That Were Actually Worth It

New Pennies Abroad · Oct 4, 2026

I spent three days in Scotland this September, and these five things were actually worth it without breaking the bank.

While studying abroad in Ireland, I spent a long weekend in Glasgow with friends. We flew from Dublin into Glasgow early Friday morning and headed home early Sunday morning, so we packed a lot into a short trip.

Before you go: Don't forget to apply for your UK Electronic Travel Authorisation (ETA). You can't board your flight without it, and it can take up to three days to process. For my group, it was approved instantly, but don't leave it to the last minute.

1. Glasgow Cathedral & Necropolis (Free)

Castle St, Glasgow G4 0QZ, United Kingdom

This stunning cathedral is the oldest on mainland Scotland, and it was a fascinating place to explore. After our visit, we headed up to the Necropolis next door, a Victorian graveyard packed with impressive monuments. It also has the best view of the city.

Tip: Be ready for a bit of a climb to reach the top. It's totally worth it.

2. Sugo Pasta (£10–15 per dish)

70 Mitchell St, Glasgow G1 3LX, United Kingdom

This Italian spot was hands-down incredible. All of the pasta is made from scratch, and the ingredients for each dish are sourced from different regions of Italy. It beat plenty of pricier Italian restaurants I've been to, for a fraction of the cost.

Tip: Sugo is always packed and doesn't take reservations, so expect a wait. Trust me, it's worth it.

3. Revolution Bar (£2–10 per drink)

84 Mitchell St, Glasgow G1 3NA, United Kingdom

This one is for the students. Revolution in Glasgow offers a student Party Pass that gets you discounted drinks: shots are £2 and pints are £4.

Getting one is easy. Fill out a short online form, show the confirmation page to the host, and they'll hand you your pass. You can apply for the Revolution Student Party Pass here.

Note: Please drink responsibly. This is a money-saving tip, not an encouragement to overdo it.

4. Toby Jug (£1.50–5 per pint)

97 Hope St, Glasgow G2 6LL, United Kingdom

Toby Jug is a popular Glasgow bar with unbeatable drink deals, especially on beer, plus karaoke. A bottle of beer was £1.50, a pint was £2.20, and cider was £2.50. We stumbled upon it by accident and liked it so much we went back a second time before the weekend was over.

5. Kelvingrove Art Gallery and Museum (Free)

Argyle St, Glasgow G3 8AG, United Kingdom

This art museum is an absolute must-see. Spread across three floors, the collection is packed with remarkable pieces, from Picasso to Dalí. The museum also hosts a half-hour organ recital every day at 1 PM, which was a highlight of the visit.

It's a great family-friendly stop, and it felt very safe.

Final Thoughts

Three days in Glasgow proved you don't need a big budget to have an unforgettable trip. Two of my five favourite stops were completely free, the best meal of the weekend cost less than £15, and the student deals kept our nights out cheap.

If you're studying abroad in Ireland, Glasgow is one of the easiest weekend trips you can take: a short flight from Dublin, a walkable city, and plenty to see without overspending. Just remember to sort out your ETA before you go, bring comfortable shoes for the Necropolis climb, and leave room in your stomach for Sugo.
