Country by country

Travel tips by destination

Here are must-know tips that will make saving money while you travel easy. Every penny counts!

Spain

ES
Currency: Euro (€)Visa: required for non-EU stays over 90 days
  • The "menu del día" at lunch gets you a full multi-course meal for a fraction of the dinner price.
  • A monthly transit pass (abono) beats single tickets after about 10–12 rides.
  • Look for piso-share listings through university boards before paying agency fees.

Italy

IT
Currency: Euro (€)Visa: required for non-EU stays over 90 days
  • Standing at the bar for coffee is cheaper than sitting at a table — ask for "al banco."
  • Regional trains cost far less than high-speed ones if you're not in a hurry.
  • Markets (mercati) undercut supermarkets badly on produce and are worth the early start.

United Kingdom

GB
Currency: Pound sterling (£)Visa: student visa required for most non-UK/EU nationals
  • A 16-25 Railcard cuts most train fares by a third and pays for itself in one trip.
  • Contactless capping on buses and the Tube usually beats buying a paper day pass.
  • Supermarket "reduced" sections near closing time are a reliable way to cut grocery costs.

France

FR
Currency: Euro (€)Visa: required for non-EU stays over 90 days
  • University restaurants (CROUS) offer full meals at a fixed, subsidized student rate.
  • Book SNCF trains as early as they open for booking — prices climb steadily after that.
  • A student CAF housing subsidy can cover part of the rent — apply as soon as you sign a lease.

Japan

JP
Currency: Japanese yen (¥)Visa: student visa required for most stays over 90 days
  • Convenience-store meals (konbini) are cheap, fast, and better than their reputation suggests.
  • An IC card for trains and buses saves the hassle of single tickets and works almost everywhere.
  • 100-yen shops cover most dorm-room basics for a fraction of department-store prices.

About

Hi, I'm Juliana

Hi, I'm Juliana, the founder of Pennies Abroad.

I grew up in New York, went to school in Florida, and in 2025 I took the leap to study abroad in Rome, Italy, where I lived, explored, and fell in love with travel on less than $2,000 (flights not included). These days, that study-abroad adventure continues in County Waterford, Ireland. Budgeting has always been second nature to me, and it's the very thing that made these experiences possible in the first place.

I created Pennies Abroad because I know too many students talk themselves out of studying abroad, convinced it's simply out of reach financially. It doesn't have to be. This is a place for honest, practical budget tips and real advice, built to help students and young travelers like you stop letting cost stand between you and the experience of a lifetime.
